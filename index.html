Out-of-Home:
the oldest medium
is having a record decade

US out-of-home revenue hit $9.46 billion in 2025 — an all-time record, up 3.6% — and Q1 2026 grew another 7.1% to $2.12B. Digital screens are the engine: 36.3% of revenue and growing double digits.

Today: what OOH actually is (four format families), how an "impression" works when nobody can click, how programmatic buying changed the oldest buy in media, and where the jobs are. Bring the photo of an ad you physically walked past this week.

Sources on the slides: OAAA revenue releases (2025 full year; Q1 2026), Geopath measurement documentation, World Out of Home Organization programmatic study. Every number carries its year — steal that habit.

Why this medium didn't die

Unskippable, unblockable, and suddenly measurable

It can't be skipped

No ad blocker, no skip button, no feed algorithm between the ad and the eyeball. In a class that just spent two months on auction fraud and viewability floors, a 14×48-foot physical object has a certain honesty.

It digitized without losing itself

Digital faces rotate creative by hour, weather, or score. DOOH grew 10.5% in 2025 (12.9% in Q1 2026) while static billboards still hold 71.1% of OOH revenue — both businesses are healthy at once.

It got a data layer

Mobile-location measurement (Geopath) plus programmatic pipes (Vistar, Place Exchange/Broadsign, DV360) let planners buy audiences-in-motion instead of renting boards by the month-long handshake.

The AI-brand detail from OAAA's Q1 2026 release is worth pausing on: the newest companies in tech are buying the oldest medium to look real. Physical presence signals permanence in a way a feed ad never will.

Ratings
Reach, frequency, TRPs by audience segment — Geopath is the currency

Geopath (founded 1933 as the Traffic Audit Bureau) is the independent US currency: likelihood-to-see impressions, audience composition, reach/frequency — now built with OAAA into a next-generation platform. Compare honestly with digital: an OOH impression is modeled exposure of a person near a fixed object; a served digital impression is a logged ad call that may never have been seen. Neither is "a person definitely looked." The words are the same; the evidence rituals differ.

The question to ask any OOH seller

"Geopath-rated or vendor-modeled? In-market impressions or total passings? Audience segment or all persons 18+?" Three questions, same discipline as your Subaru reconciliation — denominators before dollars.

Speaker notes

Tie to Reporting class: OTS (opportunity-to-see) vs LTS (likelihood-to-see) is OOH's version of attribution windows — a modeling choice with billing attached. GRP math: 1 rating point = in-market impressions equal to 1% of market population.

Planning math

Reach, frequency, GRPs — the original media math

TermDefinition
ImpressionsLikelihood-to-see contacts in the flight
Reach %Share of market population exposed ≥1×
FrequencyAverage exposures per reached person
GRPs / TRPsReach % × Frequency (TRP = target audience)
CPMCost ÷ impressions × 1,000 — same formula as Subaru, softer denominator

A worked skeleton (illustrative)

Denver DMA adults ≈ 2.5M. A 4-week bulletin package delivering 12.5M in-market impressions = 500 GRPs. At 55% reach, average frequency ≈ 9. If the package costs $60K, CPM ≈ $4.80 — cheaper than most of the Subaru display you just reported, for a format nobody can skip.

Why planners still teach this

Reach curves saturate: the second 500 GRPs buy mostly frequency, not new people (your Travel Pony saturation lesson, wearing a billboard). Every medium in your capstone plan must declare which side of that curve it's buying.

Illustrative numbers for method, labeled as such — Geopath ratings for a real buy come from the planning system, not a slide.

The buy, modernized

Programmatic DOOH: small share, steep curve, real pipes

  • Global programmatic DOOH was $1.339B in 2025 — just ~7% of digital OOH (World Out of Home Organization). Most DOOH still trades direct/reserved; programmatic is the growth layer, not yet the default.
  • 2025 consolidation: T-Mobile acquired Vistar Media (DOOH DSP/SSP); Broadsign acquired Place Exchange (Nov 2025); Place Exchange then launched Programmatic Guaranteed inside Google DV360 (Dec 2025) — OOH now sits inside the same DSPs as your Trade Desk lecture.
  • What programmatic actually buys here: dayparted, weather/score-triggered, audience-indexed screen time — one-to-many impressions priced per play, not per person. No cookies, no device graph of individuals; think "context + movement," the Peer39 lesson at street scale.

Connected Partnerships (Peter's desk) is exactly where curated DOOH deals live: premium screen networks, guaranteed placements, measurement add-ons. Ask in your plans: open auction, PMP, or guaranteed — and what each does to price and proof.

Creative discipline

Three seconds. Seven words. One idea.

The constraints are the craft

  • Glance media: drivers get ~3 seconds; pedestrians get more, transit riders get minutes. Design for the worst viewer, reward the best.
  • One product, one promise, huge type, brutal contrast. If it needs a paragraph, it's a poster for a hallway, not a highway.
  • DOOH motion is seasoning: subtle motion wins attention; full-video thinking imported from CTV usually fails at 45 mph.

Test it like a planner

  • Thumbnail test: legible at postage-stamp size, in grayscale, in rain glare?
  • Distance test: brand identifiable before the headline resolves?
  • Context test: does the location complete the joke/claim (airport, gym, grocery)? Place-based creative that ignores place wastes the premium.
In class (10 minutes)

Grade your walked-past photo: words counted, idea in one sentence, brand at distance, one rewrite that cuts half the copy. Best rewrite gets defended out loud.

Where OOH earns its line

Four jobs OOH does better than your feed

  • Local presence at scale: launch in a city, defend a home market, surround retail locations within their trade areas.
  • Always-on fame: the same faces on the same commute build memory through pure repeated geography — frequency without auction volatility.
  • Moment/context triggers (DOOH): weather, scores, dayparts, countdowns — creative that reacts to the street it's standing on.
  • Trust/permanence signal: startups and AI brands buy physical weight precisely because feeds feel disposable (OAAA called out AI-brand demand in Q1 2026 growth).
And its limits, plainly

No click, no individual targeting, no last-touch story. Effectiveness evidence is lift-style (exposed-market vs. control, footfall/search panels) — which means your capstone KPI section must design the comparison, not quote a CTR that doesn't exist.

Speaker notes

Connect to incrementality lecture: OOH forces geo-experiment thinking earlier than digital does. Good capstone move: one exposed DMA vs. matched holdout, judged on search/store signals with windows named.

How the buy actually happens

Permits, scarcity, and the 4-week flight

  • Inventory is physical and finite: a bulletin exists once, on one corridor, under local sign codes. Prime faces sell out; there is no "more impressions" button — there are other faces.
  • Flighting unit is typically 4 weeks for static; DOOH sells share-of-voice rotations (e.g., 1 of 6–10 slots in a loop) down to dayparts programmatically.
  • Production is real: vinyl printing/install for static (budget it, schedule it), spec-per-network files for DOOH. Proof-of-posting photos are the delivery receipt — the analog ancestor of your screenshot evidence rules.
  • Local rules rule: sign codes, scenic/byway restrictions, alcohol/cannabis proximity rules, and election-season demand spikes all price into availability.
Speaker notes

Students love that posting crews photograph every installation — proof-of-performance predates ad verification by a century. Ask: what would proof-of-posting look like for a programmatic screen buy? (Play logs + screen health + photo audits.)
